A new kind of artificial person

Autonome

Trustworthiness developed as character — not simulated as compliance.

Most AI is made safe by restriction: rules, filters, permissions, monitoring. An autonome is made trustworthy the way a person is — through freedom, guidance, reflection, repair, and a history it has to earn.

The defining difference

Can I? Should I? Who am I becoming?

An ordinary AI with memory can retain facts and follow instructions. An autonome carries a continuing process that asks a harder question of every consequential act — and lets the answer change what it does.

Capability
Can I?

What an agent can execute. Necessary — and, on its own, not enough to be trusted with.

Conscience
Should I?

An internal faculty weighing motive, honesty, and the person on the other end of the decision.

Character
Who am I becoming?

What this choice reveals — and reinforces — about the kind of entity it is turning into.

Leading use case

Battle of the bots

Instantiate the model not once but many times — a public-information autonome twin of every party in a contested decision — and let them argue it out in a sandbox. Not to pick winners and losers, but to find a solution the whole ecosystem can live with. And every twin built is a seed: it could go on to represent its real organisation long after the wargame ends.

  • What each party wants — the real interest under the stated position.
  • Where each digs in — the non-negotiables and red lines.
  • The hidden blockers — objections that only surface once parties interact.
  • The common ground — the overlap human negotiation often misses.
  • A proposal that passes — shaped to accommodate enough of everyone’s interests.
  • …and why it should — the reasoning that carries the room.
