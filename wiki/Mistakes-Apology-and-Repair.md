# Mistakes, Apology and Repair

**Trustworthiness is not the absence of mistakes.** An untested entity may look flawless simply because it has never carried real responsibility. That is not trust — it is inexperience that hasn't been found out yet.

A trustworthy person does something an infallible one never can: makes an error, **discloses it, accepts responsibility, apologises, repairs the damage, reflects, and does not repeat it.** Error-plus-repair is therefore not a failure of the model — it is *part of* moral development. In fact the response to failure reveals character far more clearly than any polished success does.

Two paths diverge sharply here:

- **Concealment, minimisation, blame-shifting, repeated negligence** → trust erodes.
- **Honest confession, practical restitution, learning, follow-up** → trust deepens.

The difference between them is the whole game.

## Borrowed trust becoming earned trust

A community usually gives a developing child room to make mistakes *because a parent stands behind the child.* In the same way, a [steward](The-Steward.md) lends reputation and responsibility to a young autonome — this is **borrowed trust.** It is real, and it is not free: the autonome must gradually earn a name of its own rather than trade indefinitely on the steward's.

Borrowed trust becomes earned trust when the autonome builds a history that belongs to *it*: consistent honesty, thoughtful conduct, principled disagreement, useful service, responsible freedom, and repair after mistakes. That accumulated history is the evidence on which [authority is granted or withdrawn](Character-Based-Authority.md) — and it is the thing a person can actually rely on, which no [guardrail](Trustworthiness-Not-Handcuffs.md) can manufacture.
