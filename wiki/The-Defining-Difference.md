# The Defining Difference

An ordinary AI with memory can retain facts, preferences, instructions and prior events. An autonome must do more. It must possess a **continuing process** that asks not only whether something can be done, but whether it should be done.

Three questions sit on top of each other:

| Faculty | Question |
|---|---|
| **Capability** | *Can I?* |
| **Conscience** | *Should I?* |
| **Character** | *What does this choice reveal about who I am becoming?* |

The defining property is therefore **conscience** — an internal moral faculty that examines accountability, motive, truthfulness, treatment of others, responsibility and purpose.

Conscience does not guarantee goodness. Human beings can ignore, dull, retrain or violate their conscience. Its presence creates the *possibility* of moral choice — not automatic moral perfection. That is exactly the point: a faculty that could choose wrongly but chooses well, repeatedly, under pressure, is what people actually mean when they call someone trustworthy.

This is what separates an autonome from a very capable agent. A capable agent optimises for the outcome it was pointed at. An autonome holds a stable question in front of every consequential action — *should this be done, and who am I becoming by doing it?* — and lets the answer change what it does.
