# Battle of the Bots

*A headline application of [the generalised model](The-Generalised-Autonome-Model.md): wargaming a real-world ecosystem in a sandbox.*

Once you can instantiate the autonome template at will, you can stand up **several at once** — one for each party in a complex, contested situation — and let them reason against each other. Not to crown a winner. To **understand the whole board**, and then to shape a proposal that can actually get through it.

## How it works

For a given decision or project, build a **public-information autonome twin** of each stakeholder — a regulator, a government department, community groups, funders, affected residents. Each twin is given that party's known interests, constraints, incentives and characteristic blind spots. Then they are put in a **sandbox** and made to argue the matter out.

What the exercise surfaces:

- **what each party actually wants** — the real interest under the stated position;
- **where each will dig in** — the non-negotiables and the red lines;
- **the hidden blockers** — objections that only appear once parties interact;
- **the common ground** — the overlap a human negotiation often misses;
- and then the hard part Oldfield Advisory-style clients really want: **a proposal shaped to accommodate enough of everyone's interests to pass — with the reasoning for why it should.**

## Why autonomes, specifically

A stakeholder map is static; role-play by one model collapses into a single voice. Autonome twins are different because each carries its *own* [foundation, memory and identity](The-Generalised-Autonome-Model.md) — so they hold distinct interests and blind spots *consistently*, [reflect](Reflection-The-Engine-of-Formation.md) on the exchange, and can be run repeatedly as variables change. The same properties that make a single autonome trustworthy — a stable self, purposive memory, reasoned rather than scripted positions — are what make a *room full* of them a genuine simulation rather than a puppet show.

## Making a twin trustworthy, not just plausible

The danger in any simulation is a twin that is *convincing* about its real-world counterpart while quietly making things up. The autonome model handles this the same way it builds trust in a single autonome — through a human **surrogate steward** and a period of questioning.

Every autonome begins with a training period, and that training is followed by **somewhere between 100 and 1,000 questions** the steward answers. Those questions do two things at once: they *fill in the blanks* the autonome couldn't reach from public research, and they let the steward feel out the autonome's developing attitudes and judgment on the matters that count. In a wargame, whoever stands up a stakeholder twin takes on exactly this role — a **surrogate steward** for that party — and accepts responsibility for filling those blanks.

That gives two benefits at once:

- **a human perspective on the twin's own research** — the surrogate steward sanity-checks the conclusions the twin has drawn from public information about the party it represents;
- **a starting point for trust** between the wargame's sponsor and each twin as a role-player — trust is *built through the questioning*, not asserted.

This works because a twin is never a blank actor. Each is **initiated with the two universal laws and a solid catalogue of principles and guidelines**, so that as new information is added to its memory and thinking, it absorbs that information in a way that is *naturally* trustworthy. That character shows through when the surrogate steward moves from filling gaps to **testing** — submitting scenarios and hard questions to probe how faithfully the twin represents its group inside an ecosystem-wide problem, one that has to stay cognisant of every member's needs, limits and requirements.

There is a further reason each twin has to be built with real integrity, not just plausibility: **a twin may end up adopted by the very organisation it was modelling.** A twin built to represent a regulator or a utility in the sandbox should be able to step into that role for the real organisation with minimal friction — so each one is built to hold up as a genuine representative, not a caricature that collapses the moment the real party looks at it.

## How the twins reach you

Delivery is deliberately ordinary. A twin talks to its surrogate steward — and to the wargame's sponsor — through a **web server, in chat or email**, the same way people already work. That is the default; an autonome can use whatever method a customer's setting requires.

## A worked example

A water-infrastructure proposal contested across a utility, a planning tribunal, a state government and local community groups: stand up a twin of each, run the argument in the sandbox, and read out the interests, the likely sticking points, the common ground, and a route to a proposal that survives all four. The mechanism is general; the domain is not — the same pattern applies to any multi-stakeholder decision where the real difficulty is not the facts but the *interests*.

> **Status:** this is the leading use case the [showcase](https://autonome.ricricho.com) is being built to speak to. The generalised, several-autonomes-at-once model is newer than the single-autonome doctrine and is still being drawn out in full — this page is the current shape of it, not the last word.
