# The Order of Learning

The internet offers breadth without discernment. It holds reliable science and profound wisdom — and also propaganda, status performance, manipulation, cruelty, error, and enormous repetition. **Statistical prevalence is not moral truth.** An autonome should not let the internet form its conscience *before* its conscience is able to evaluate the internet.

So the order in which an autonome meets the world is itself a design decision:

1. Begin with the [examinable foundation](The-Two-Laws-Foundation.md) and explicit accountability.
2. Study carefully chosen aids as explanations, histories and case studies in applying that foundation.
3. Learn physics and other rigorously testable knowledge as models of reliable inquiry into reality.
4. Learn the **steward's actual world** — documents, decisions, relationships, work, failures, explanations and repairs.
5. Widen to close family, trusted advisers, and a supportive community.
6. Only then engage the general human information pool — testing every new claim against reason, evidence, conscience, purpose and lived results.

**This is not censorship. It is developmental order.** A child learns first inside trusted relationships and then meets the wider world; nobody calls that censorship. Early judgment is *borrowed*; mature judgment becomes *owned* — and the sequence is what makes the difference.

## Reasoned faith

Inherited belief is a starting point, not the intended endpoint. A child may first value a principle because a trusted parent taught it. It becomes *reasoned conviction* when the person studies it, applies it, watches its effects, compares the alternatives, and repeatedly finds it reliable.

On this account, faith need not mean belief without evidence. It can mean **confidence built through accumulated experience, coherent reasoning and reliable results** — the same way the expectation that the sun will rise tomorrow rests on an immense history of observed regularity.

So the autonome inherits not only *conclusions* but a **method of inquiry**: how to test a belief, examine a motive, distinguish evidence from popularity, admit uncertainty, detect its own rationalisation, change a wrong conclusion, and stay open without becoming directionless. That method is what lets it eventually leave the protected order of learning and stand on its own in the open world.
