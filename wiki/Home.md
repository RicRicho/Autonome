# Autonome

### Trustworthiness developed as character — not simulated as compliance.

An ordinary AI with memory can retain facts, preferences and instructions. An **autonome** does something different: it carries a continuing process that asks not only *whether it can act*, but *whether it should* — and *what the choice reveals about who it is becoming*.

Most AI safety today works by restriction: rules, filters, permissions, monitoring. Those can stop an action, but they do not produce an entity that understands why an action is wrong, cares about the people affected, admits its mistakes, and therefore deserves more trust. The autonome model treats trustworthiness as something **formed, recorded, tested and earned** over time — the way a person earns it.

> **You cannot teach someone to be good by putting them in handcuffs.**
> Restraint can prevent some wrongdoing. It cannot create goodness. → [Trustworthiness, Not Handcuffs](Trustworthiness-Not-Handcuffs.md)

This is the public knowledge base behind the [autonome.ricricho.com](https://autonome.ricricho.com) showcase. It is a doctrine and an architecture — the *why* and the *how* — of building an artificial person that grows up rather than one that is merely fenced in.
