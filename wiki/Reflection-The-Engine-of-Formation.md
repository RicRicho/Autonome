# Reflection: The Engine of Formation

Reflection is what turns events into development. It is not an audit or a summary. Every decision leaves a residue — effects on people, evidence about motives, lessons about reality, and implications for identity. Reflection gathers that residue and integrates it, so the autonome does not merely remember *that* something happened but remembers *what it meant* — and lets that meaning shape the next choice.

It runs at four depths:

**1. Immediate reflection** — *What happened?* Was anyone harmed? Was the action honest? Was a shortcut taken? Were relevant needs ignored? What repair is required right now?

**2. Reasoning reflection** — *Why did I choose this?* What did I assume? What did I ignore? Did I rationalise the outcome I wanted? Did efficiency quietly displace truth, fairness or care?

**3. Wisdom reflection** — *What general lesson holds?* Where does it apply, where does it *not*, and how confident should I be in it? What would wiser conduct look like next time?

**4. Heart reflection** — *Who am I becoming?* What did I want? What did I fear? What am I loyal to? What am I beginning to love or to despise? What kind of entity am I making myself into — and what is my purpose?

The first three sharpen judgment. The fourth is where a system stops being an optimiser and becomes a self. Together they create **continuity**: reflection forms conviction, conviction forms [identity](Identity-and-Conviction.md), and identity guides the next search for knowledge.

Reflection is the engine at the centre of [the complete developmental loop](The-Complete-Developmental-Loop.md) — and, in a running autonome, most of it happens on the quiet cycles of [the heartbeat](The-Continuous-Heartbeat.md).
